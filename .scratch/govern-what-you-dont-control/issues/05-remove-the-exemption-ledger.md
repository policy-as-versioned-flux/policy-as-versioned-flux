# 05 — Remove the exemption ledger, and let the cage produce its evidence

Type: task
Status: open
Blocked by: 04

## Question

Exemptions are a banned concept (see the map's Notes and `CONTEXT.md`). The estate ships one anyway:
`estate/platform/policy/ledger/exemptions.yaml` is a live `ResourceSet` rendering a real
`PolicyException` per entry, with `render-exemption.py` as its offline twin, `verify-exemption.sh` as
a passing beat, and `EXC-2026-001` exempting `legacy-till` from `may-run-root-if-attested`.

**Take the estate's own defence seriously before deleting it.** The ledger's comments argue every
entry is *scoped* (a CEL predicate, not a namespace-wide hole), *priced* (a FAIR scenario whose
residual ALE is the £ it carries), *expiring* (a TTL on the rendered exception), *versioned* and
*owned* — and that it exists only for one-offs that cannot be expressed as a uniform conditional
branch. That is a good argument, and it is overruled: an entry naming one workload is a carve-out
whatever properties it carries.

**What replaces it, decided:**
- **Compliance is the cage's job.** A workload that cannot meet the condition gets a cage that
  implements the control on its behalf; the residual is priced into the institution's band.
- **The cage produces the OSCAL `risk` object**, taking over from `render-exemption.py`. The cage
  already knows the tier, the residual and the workload — which is exactly what the risk object
  needs — so evidence is generated by the thing actually constraining the workload rather than by a
  document asserting an intention. **Do not remove the ledger before this producer exists**, or the
  OSCAL up-flow beat loses its input.
- **Some workloads will stop running**, and that is accepted. `legacy-till` cannot meet even the
  compensating condition (`readOnlyRootFilesystem` + drop ALL), and under the cage model those are
  imposed regardless — which may break it. If no acceptable cage lets it run, it does not run;
  refusal is the bottom rung reached by the £, not a carve-out.
- **Surface it at procurement, not at admission.** Discovering "this cannot run inside any cage we
  would accept" at admission is late and expensive; the procurement record is where it belongs.

**Consequences to handle, not discover:**
1. `estate/talk/verify-all.sh:25` loses the **"no ledger entry, no exception"** beat
   (`verify-exemption.sh`). The headline count changes and the multi-org map's 28/28 target moves
   with it — coordinate rather than surprise it. Line 24's *"exemptions dissolve into conditional
   policy"* beat (`verify-conditional.sh`) **stays**: that is the sanctioned mechanism, and after
   this change its name becomes literally true rather than aspirational.
2. The OSCAL up-flow beat depends on the ledger's risk objects; sequence the cage producer first.
4. Check nothing else reads `ledger/` or `render-exemption.py` before deleting.
