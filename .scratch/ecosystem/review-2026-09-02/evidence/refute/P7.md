## Where the finding is wrong

### ADR-0023 does not belong in the list

Every bullet in ADR-0023's `## The decision` section is one of D1–D5, the five cross-ticket
decisions the estate's own record marks **DECIDED**, not provisional:

| ADR-0023 bullet | Decision | Where the ticket marks it DECIDED |
|---|---|---|
| clock may append observations, never a declaration | D1 | `10-schedules-and-skills.md:86`, `16-...:76` |
| fetch opens a PR only when the bump is not `none` | D2 | `10-schedules-and-skills.md:88` |
| gitsign tag is the only signature; verifying controller at the Flux source boundary | D3 | `16-flux-rescoped-...:78` ("Q4, decided, D3") |
| step-4 number comes only from the scheduled CI run | D4 | `16-...:82`, `20-one-demo-...:77` ("Decided.") |
| a pin behind a newer version is priced by the EOL ramp; `revoked[]` stays withdrawal | D5 | `13-lift-or-retire-...:83` ("Decided."), `19-misuse-...:84` |

`10-schedules-and-skills.md:80` states the split in one sentence: items 1–5 of that ticket "are
PROVISIONAL" on the bare agree, then "The cross-ticket conflicts were then put to the owner as five
decisions with a three-lens panel verdict. The owner wrote: \"I agree with you're more advanced
reasoning\". Amendments D1, D2 and D5 are DECIDED on that line."
`map.md:18` says the same: "those five are decided (D1 to D5 in each ticket's Answer, ADR-0023)."
And ADR-0024:84 calls it "the ratified D5 ... (ADR-0023)".

ADR-0023 is therefore the batch's **decided** ADR. Its silence on "provisional" is correct, not an
omission. Worse for the finding, ADR-0023 is the *most* self-disclosing of the six: lines 9-10 name
the three-lens panel and quote the owner's ratifying words verbatim ("I agree with you're more
advanced reasoning"), and lines 12-38 carry a dated Amendment admitting the gate contradicts D3
today. Counting it as an omission conflates the ratified half of the batch with the provisional
half — the exact distinction the finding is complaining is not visible.

### The ambition reference is misattributed

NORTH-STAR §6 is "What is explicitly out" (talk/videos, power layer, covert sensing, real
surveillance data, the original org). It says nothing about the record being an honesty surface.
§5 is the truth surface and is about verify scripts and three-outcome live tails. The nearest real
hook is §3 **principle 6** (`NORTH-STAR.md:35`): "Every actor is attestable, and the record is
falsifiable. Every artefact carries a signature that says what it does and does not assert" — an
analogy, not a rule about ADR prose. `map.md:16`'s process rule reads "a decision is recorded with
the owner's reason or it stays open", which the batch satisfied by recording the recommendation's
own reasoning as rationale; it does not mandate the word in the ADR file. The only place that
mandate is written down is `map.md:18` and ADR-0024:85's "per the map's process rule".

### "Lives only in the ticket tracker" overstates

The finding says the convention "lives only in `.scratch/`", then in the same paragraph cites the
two `docs/adr/` files that carry it. Both are true: it reaches the durable record twice
(0022, 0024) and misses it three times. Each of the three silent ADRs also carries a pointer to its
ticket on its line 9 ("Decided 2026-08-28 in `.scratch/ecosystem/issues/04`"), so the caveat is
traceable in one hop — just not self-disclosing.

## Why the gap is real anyway

`git log -S "Provisional: the owner agreed" -- docs/adr/0022-*.md` → `20568bc 2026-08-28`, the
batch commit that also created ADR-0023. ADR-0019 was created earlier the same day in its own
ticket commit (`6ee916a 2026-08-28 ecosystem: ticket 04, the feed contract`), and ADR-0024's
disclosure arrived in the ticket-28 build commit `7b66fa3`. The convention was adopted mid-batch
and applied forward; it was never backported to the three ADRs written before it. That is a real,
observable inconsistency in the canonical record, and it lands on ADR-0019 (the feed envelope every
publisher and adopter implements) and ADR-0020 (the refuse-vs-price doctrine), which is what makes
it worth keeping.

## Not a blind re-raise

`REVIEW-2026-08-31.md:99` refutes a neighbouring claim — "17 of 22 decisions rest on a bare agree
with no route to ratification" — on the grounds that the provisional convention is a recorded
process rule with a reopen path. P7 is a narrower and different claim (the *disclosure* in
`docs/adr/`, not the *existence* of a ratification route), so it is not the refuted claim
re-raised. But the earlier refutation does bear on severity: the route exists and the record is
traceable, so this is a record-hygiene defect, not a governance hole. Major reads high to me;
minor-to-moderate is the honest grade.
