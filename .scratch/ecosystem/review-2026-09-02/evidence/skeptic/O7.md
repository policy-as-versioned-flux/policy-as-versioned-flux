## Corrections the finding needs

**C1. "byte-identical" is false.** `scripts/lib.sh` has three distinct md5s
(`fbd2a2eb…`, `49e4c453…`, `d273fe7f…`); the `kind/` directories do not even share filenames
(`driftwood.yaml` / `tuppence.yaml` / `ludlow.yaml`). They are *identical modulo the adopter's own
name*: the lib.sh diff is 5 substituted lines (CLUSTER, HERE comment, IMAGE, GIT_URL_IN_CLUSTER,
header), the kind yaml diff is 1 line (`name:`). Likewise `composed/policies/v4.0.0/*.yaml` differ
in exactly one line each (`policy-as-versioned.dev/composed-for: <adopter>`), so they too are not
byte-identical.

**C2. "copy-pasted" mischaracterises the gates.** Sorted-line intersection between the gates is
260 (driftwood∩tuppence), 340 (driftwood∩ludlow), 261 (tuppence∩ludlow) lines out of 661-1213.
These read as three independent implementations of one ticket, with different CLI surfaces
(driftwood: subcommands `read-pin`/`verify-commit`/`compose`; tuppence: one flag-driven entrypoint;
ludlow: PR-base/head pin reading) and different function counts (23/20/30 `def`s). The estate's own
word for it is "evolved independently", which is more accurate than "copy-pasted".

**C3. The ambition reference is misapplied.** NORTH-STAR:33 (principle 4) is about *policy* being a
pinned, signed, composed dependency — and that layer genuinely is shared (the composed policy trees
differ by one label line). The gap is in the CI/verification apparatus. Moreover platform declares
that layer institution-owned on purpose:
`platform/shift-left/ci-workflow.example.yml:1-3` — "Not run here -- each institution owns its own
.github/workflows/; this is the reference shape ci-check.py expects." So the honest statement is
that the README's unqualified sentence reads wider than what is actually inherited, and that the
deliberately institution-owned CI layer has no owner for its drift — not that principle 4 is
contradicted.

**C4. "the largest single obstacle" is an unverifiable superlative.** Drop it; no comparative
measurement was made against the other findings.

## Additions that strengthen the finding

**A1. The divergence has a substantive security consequence, not just a size one.** Only ludlow
pins the Sigstore trust root: `ludlow/.github/scripts/trusted_root.json` exists, and
`adopter_gate.py:109-111,339-342` passes `--trusted-root=<committed file>` and
`--new-bundle-format=true`. driftwood (`adopter-gate.py:255-256`) and tuppence
(`adopter-gate.py:246-248`) invoke `cosign verify-blob` with `--bundle`,
`--certificate-identity-regexp` and `--certificate-oidc-issuer` only, so trust material resolves via
TUF rather than a committed root — while tuppence's own docstring (`:209`) calls the check
"offline". cs-28's acceptance criterion is "verifies the publisher's evidence signature,
identity-pinned **and offline**"; three implementations, one of which implements the offline half.
(Observed in source; I did not run cosign.)

**A2. The driftwood `./apps` bug sits in a lane that runs against the real remote.**
`driftwood/.github/workflows/drift-sample.yml:158-161` applies the committed pins unedited —
"gotk-sync.yaml and gotk-sync-nist.yaml point at https://github.com/policy-as-versioned-*/*  …
scripts/up.sh's git-server rewrite is never applied here" — then `kubectl apply -f
gitops/flux-system/`. That is exactly the case the tuppence/ludlow comment says never becomes Ready.
It is invisible to the instrument: every sample in all three `drift/samples.jsonl` files is scoped
`party: nist, consumer: verified-source-only`, so no fact reads the adopter's own Kustomization.
I could not observe a live NotReady condition (read-only review, no cluster).

**A3. The shared-artefact shape existed in the 2022 org and was not carried forward.**

`{"archived":false,"description":"Composite Action: verify a policy-version bump PR's trust chain
before merge","name":"pr-gate-action","pushedAt":"2026-07-16T12:35:29Z"}`. The estate that replaced
it ships the same responsibility three times as repo-local Python.
