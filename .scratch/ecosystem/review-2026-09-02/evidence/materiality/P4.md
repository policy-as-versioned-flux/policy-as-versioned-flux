# Materiality: P4 (participants) — unpinned platform checkout in three publisher release gates

Verdict: MATERIAL. Severity: major (confirms the finding's own grade, for a partly different reason).

Reframed: "A publisher's signature attests a release gate nobody can reconstruct — three
publishers decide what they may assert using whatever platform main said that day."

## Why it is material, not pedantry

1. It is the "up" half of principle 4, which is the thesis's whole claim.
   NORTH-STAR principle 4: "Policy is a versioned dependency, all the way up and down.
   Regulators, intelligence providers and the platform each version and sign what they
   publish." The pinning discipline is demonstrated on the adopter side. On the publisher
   side, at the exact moment a publisher mints a signed artefact, the discipline is absent.
   The half of the model that is hardest to believe (that publishers behave like package
   maintainers) is the half not shown.

2. What is consumed is contract, not lint. Verified in-session:
   - ico/verify-penalty-feed.sh:21  `fair="$here/../platform/fair/fair.py"` — the £ engine
   - feeds/verify-feeds.sh:22-32     platform/feeds/schema.json — the ADR-0019 envelope
   - insurer/verify-insurer-party.sh:83 `${PLATFORM_DIR:-../platform}/party/party_artefact.py`
     validating party.yaml against platform/party/schema.json
   NORTH-STAR §2: "The only shared things are the artefact contracts and the £." These three
   ARE the artefact contracts and the £, consumed from a moving branch. The defence "it's only
   CI tooling" does not survive reading the scripts.

3. It degrades what a signed publisher tag means (principle 6). A gitsign-verified publisher
   tag currently implies "this passed the release gate". Which gate is not recorded anywhere in
   the artefact and cannot be recovered later, because the gate's inputs came from an unrecorded
   platform HEAD. That is a signature asserting more than it can support, and it bears directly
   on demonstration steps 6 and 7 (provenance verifiable; honesty reported).

4. A subtler point the finding under-plays and which raises, not lowers, materiality:
   NORTH-STAR §2's Regulator row says the regulator **Consumes: Nothing**. ico's release
   decision consumes the platform's £ engine. The issue is therefore not only "unpinned" but
   a participant-model inversion: an edge the table says does not exist. Pinning ico to a
   platform tag would satisfy §2's pinning clause while leaving the Consumes column false.
   Whoever fixes this should decide which of the two is wrong, the workflow or the table.

## Why not critical

The ambition survives while it stands. The mechanism exists and demonstrably works elsewhere:
feeds/.github/workflows/cut-release.yml:56-61 pins `ref: v2.0.1` for the same schema, and every
adopter Flux pin on platform and nist is a correct tag+commit pair. Blast radius is bounded to
accept/reject: an unpinned platform HEAD can let through or wrongly block a release, but cannot
alter the content of the published artefact and cannot forge the tag signature. No divergent
gate outcome has been observed. This is a stated principle not being true at three named
participants — the definition of major here, not of critical.

## Why not minor / not a recorded scope decision

Checked for ownership:
- .scratch/ecosystem/issues/62-...md scope sentence: "Every adopter's CI checks out ico at ref:
  main and feeds/insurer at ref: ecosystem/thin-slice" — adopter-side consumption only; the
  2026-09-01 comment enumerates the twelve sites, all in tuppence/ludlow. Publisher release
  gates are not in scope.
- .scratch/ecosystem/issues/18-the-publisher-release-under-cages.md (resolved) inspects the very
  files: "ico and nist each have cut-release.yml and release.yml; release.yml runs an offline
  verify step labelled 'release gate' (ico/.github/workflows/release.yml:71 ...)" — it grades
  the absence of a bump-computing gate and never touches the platform checkout's ref.
So: no ticket owns it, and no ADR or REGRILL answer accepts it. feeds pinning in cut-release.yml
but not release.yml is internal inconsistency in one repo on the same day's discipline, which
reads as oversight rather than a decision.

## Caveats on the finding as written

- "the most coupled thing they do" is rhetoric; the coupling is CI-time and one-directional.
  The substance stands without it.
- nist being clean is correct and worth keeping in the write-up: it shows a publisher gate can
  be self-contained, so the fix for ico is not necessarily "pin platform" but possibly "stop
  depending on platform".
