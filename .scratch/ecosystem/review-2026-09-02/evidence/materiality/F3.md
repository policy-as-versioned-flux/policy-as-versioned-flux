## Why it matters for the purpose
- NORTH-STAR principle 1 and 3: an allowance is a conditional rule or a cage with a price. An adopter
  sitting on a pin behind a newer published version is today an untimed, unpriced allowance. Staleness
  is free, and the eco-system's whole claim is that nothing is free and unpriced.
- Principle 4 ("policy is a versioned dependency, pinned, bumped by reviewed PR") loses its incentive
  edge: Renovate can raise a pin, but nothing makes not raising it cost anything.
- Principle 5 ("intelligence re-prices on a clock"): the eol feed is the ONE published feed whose price
  moves with the calendar rather than with a publisher bump, and no clock passes --as-of and no adopter
  subscribes. Re-pricing today is bump-driven only. The principle is dented, not false.
- Principle 6 ("the record is falsifiable"): a DECIDED entry, restated twice in the live map, with no
  build ticket and no ADR banner, is the record asserting a capability that does not exist.

## Why not critical
- Not in the thesis, not a §1 clause, not a §2 participant, not one of §4's seven steps.
- Judged alone (F1 held aside), it removes one of ticket 74's three movers for step 3; the feed-bump
  mover and the size/appetite mover remain, so step 3 is not blocked by this finding on its own.
- NORTH-STAR §6's "lift or explicitly retire, with a decision each" is satisfied in letter: the
  consumer-side sunset WAS explicitly retired with a decision. What is missing is the replacement
  the same decision promised.

## Why not pedantry / not a recorded scope decision
The recorded decision is the opposite of a scope-out: D5 says this IS how supersede works. The gap is
between a decision marked DECIDED and any build owning it. Ticket 13's graduation list (lift apps,
handbook render, round 2) and ticket 19's (misuse catalogue, switching cost, forecast book) carry no
supersede-pricing item, and ticket 74 already depends on it. Unowned and load-bearing, not merely
not-built-yet.
