# Materiality verdict — TF-08

Material: yes. Severity: major (not critical).

Reframed: "The one dependency the thesis is named after is the only one nothing bumps —
a new policy version raises no PR anywhere, and no workload is ever pulled off a retiring version."

## Why it is material

- The thesis's headline -able is exactly this loop: research/03-blogs-thesis.md:65
  "**updatable** | Updating policy is no different to any other dependency — use Dependabot / Renovate."
  NORTH-STAR.md:21 principle 4 restates it as "Policy is a versioned dependency, all the way up and down."
  The policy line is the one line in the estate where "down" stops.
- The consumer surface the thesis describes exists and is hand-edited. driftwood/deploy/pod.yaml:31
  `labels: { "policy-as-versioned.dev/policy-version": "4.0.0" }` on a real workload (checkout-svc),
  and gitops/apps/version-configmap.yaml:13 `policyVersion: "1.0.0"`. Nothing in driftwood/renovate.json's
  four customManagers matches either. platform carries policy/v2.0.0 … policy/v4.0.0 as a distinct signed
  tag namespace (git tag -l), consumed by nobody as a pin.
- Retirement therefore has no pull side. map.md:72 "Policy versions 2.0.0, 2.0.1 and 3.0.0 are retired,
  not patched", and map.md:40 records supersede as "publisher-side only". The thesis's stated reason for
  multi-version runtime support is *"transitionary periods for old policy versions to be retired"* —
  a transition needs something that moves the consumer, and there is nothing.
- It is not a recorded scope decision. Ticket 18 (issues/18-…:30) records the fact and reasons only
  about its consequence for degraded elements ("whatever keeps a degraded element out … must act on the
  array element, in compose"). GAPS.md names Renovate twice (1.6 ico pins, 3.25 dashboard) and never this.
  No ticket owns it. The closest recorded rationale is a code comment —
  driftwood/gitops/platform/platform-pin.yaml: "driftwood does not author policy versions — it pins a
  signed release of platform and lets Flux fan the version array out" — which covers how the *array*
  arrives, not how a workload's version claim moves.

## Why not critical

- The dependency loop is not vapour in this estate: driftwood pins platform by {tag, commit} v2.0.1 under
  a Renovate customManager (renovate.json manager #2), and a real machine-raised cross-org bump has been
  merged by a human (driftwood #20, feeds/threat-register v2, 2026-09-01). Under the platform-pin.yaml
  reading, "updating policy" at org level = bumping the platform package pin, and that is genuinely wired.
- NORTH-STAR §4 step 2 ("Renovate raises the pin in one adopter") follows step 1 ("a **regulator** publishes
  a new penalty schema version"), and ticket 61 discharged precisely that with the feed bump. The finding's
  citation of §4 step 2 over-reaches; its true ambition refs are the "updatable" -able and principle 4's
  "all the way up and down". The finding survives on the narrower axis, not the one it claims.
- So the ambition is diminished, not unclaimable: a stated principle is not true as written, which is the
  major band.

## Correction to carry forward

Strengthen the finding: the sharpest missing manager is not over `policy/v*` at the adopter's *pin* level —
where the platform package tag is a defensible unit — but over the workload's own
`policy-as-versioned.dev/policy-version` claim (driftwood/deploy/pod.yaml:31), which is the thesis's
consumer surface and is a hand-typed string.
