## Grilling round 1 — drafted 2026-08-28, HELD

Drafted AFK on the owner's instruction. Adversarially checked by two reviewers. Nothing below has been put to the owner.

❓ **Q1** - **Trust-domain topology across the parties that run a cluster**: every SVID today is minted by one SPIRE under `acme.internal` on driftwood's cluster, and tuppence's flagship runs inside it (H8-01). NORTH-STAR §2 already rules out a shared root (nothing is a tenant of anything else), so the shape is per-institution with federation; the open sub-decisions are which parties get a domain and where federation is declared. Options: (a) one trust domain per party that runs a cluster (driftwood, tuppence, ludlow today; the platform only if it ever runs one), each org's SPIRE on its own cluster, federated pairwise by `ClusterFederatedTrustDomain` (two objects per pair, six in total for three domains); `trust_domain`, `bundle_endpoint` and `federates_with[]` all signed facts on the org's `party.yaml`, so trust in a counterparty is a line the party removes from its own signed artefact; (b) the same, but `federates_with` in the composed artefact and only `trust_domain`/`bundle_endpoint` on `party.yaml`, two homes; (c) one shared trust domain with nested SPIRE, the platform as root CA.

➡️ (a). Federation is a subscription to a counterparty and CONTEXT.md says the party artefact is the only subscription record; ADR-0018 keeps lists out of the composed artefact, which rules out (b). (c) is the tenant relationship §2 forbids and ADR-0012 already rejected a shared signer for the same reason. Regulators, feeds and the insurer run no workloads and get no domain. Cost: one bundle endpoint per cluster, six federation objects on CRDs already installed (`spire/helmrelease.yaml:19`).

❓ **Q2** - **What the SVID path carries, and what stamps it**: the path is `/posture/<platform-policy-version>/ns/<ns>/sa/<sa>`. The SVID is the only signed carrier that crosses a cluster boundary, so a segment absent from it is invisible to a counterparty. Two constraints bind any change: the SPIRE template reads pod labels only, and under ticket 09 Q1 (b) the tier is declared on the Namespace, so a tier segment needs a Namespace-to-pod label render first (a MutatingPolicy that reads the Namespace tier, the same shape as `stamp-posture`); and nothing today says a pod claims anything but the platform policy version, so a composed-artefact segment needs a new pod claim that no ticket has decided. Options: (a) platform policy version only (status quo); (b) platform policy version plus the cage tier, `/posture/<version>/cage/<tier>/ns/...`, the tier rendered Namespace-to-pod by a new MutatingPolicy that clobbers whatever the pod carries, the single-prefix glob on the serving side unchanged; (c) as (b) plus the adopter's composed-artefact version as a further segment, which first needs the decision "what version does a pod claim under ESLint versioning" (platform, composed, or both labels).

➡️ (b). Under "no gate, just cages" the tier is the one decision a peer would price, so it must cross the boundary. (b) is one template change plus one MutatingPolicy that mirrors `stamp-posture` for the tier, and it closes H8-03 and H8-04 together without touching what a pod claims. (c) hides the bigger decision under a template change; it goes to a later round blocked on ticket 06's "what a pod claims". Named cost: a tier move re-issues the SVID (entry gone in ~10s plus one TTL, `clusterspiffeid-posture.yaml:18-21`), the clock the currency controller is designed to rely on and currently 404s on (GAPS 2.8).

❓ **Q3** - **What a serving workload may demand of a caller across the federation boundary, and where it is declared**: the flagship demand is the literal `2.0.0` in five files (H8-05) and the trust domain is a literal in `reach.py`. Once Q1 federates and Q2 fixes the path, the demand must come from something signed, and it must be able to name a counterparty, which means one party pins another's party artefact; parent kind is closed (ticket 04), so that pin is a `feed` published under the counterparty's `publishes[]` (a free name, `party`), never a fourth kind. Options: (a) a version window only, rendered from the platform's `versions.yaml`; (b) a tier floor only; (c) both, declared per service in the serving org's own composed artefact (`requires: {caller: {trust_domain, min_tier, version_window}}`), rendered by composition into the AuthorizationPolicy and the OpenBao role like every other rendered policy; `trust_domain` resolves against the pinned counterparty party feed, never a literal.

➡️ (c). (a) keeps the platform as arbiter of what tuppence may demand of driftwood, and platform versions stop being the unit once composed sets self-version. (b) drops the currency half the owner's 2026-07-31 example asked for (an intra-org example; the cross-org reading is the north star's federation inference, H8-01 refuter). (c) puts the demand where every other adopter decision lives (ADR-0012, ADR-0018), gives it the Renovate bump path, and turns `verify-reach-secrets.sh` into a cross-check against a signed file. A caller that fails the demand loses reach to that service, priced on the caller's side as a hole in `prices[]`; its tier does not move unless its own selection policy moves it. This is the reach demand, not a gate.

❓ **Q4** - **The actor-class roots (the second ADR)**: NORTH-STAR principle 2 cages five classes: workload, human, device, model action (agent), and the twin itself. Today: workload = SPIRE SVID (real); commit/tag = per-org GitHub Actions workflow OIDC subject pinned by regexp (real, a machine subject); human = Dex static bcrypt account (narrated as the same subject as the committer, false); device = SPIRE `tpm_devid`, never attested; twin agent = HMAC shared key, named in ADR-0019/0021 and rooted nowhere; proposer = workflow Actions identity (reversal 16, settled). Options: (a) record the truth: five classes, three roots (SPIRE for workloads and devices, GitHub Actions OIDC for tags and the proposer, Dex for humans), twin agent unrooted; (b) five classes, two issuers, distinct subjects: humans log in to Pomerium via GitHub-user OAuth (the person's own account, the one GitHub records as the merger, re-grill 29), tags and proposer stay workflow subjects, the adopter's twin agent signs as the workflow OIDC identity of the adopter's twin schedule, devices attest to their org's SPIRE domain (Q1), Dex retired; (c) force one root: SPIRE issues human identity too via a login flow.

➡️ (b). Two issuers already in the estate, no directory holding one demo password, and every subject honest: the ADR says "same issuer, different subjects" and drops the "one root, one subject" sentence the Dex comment narrates. It also roots the twin agent with the one mechanism reversal 16 already chose for the proposer, so ticket 11 inherits a decision rather than a gap. (c) has no trodden path for interactive login and adds a second identity per person. (a) is honest but leaves the twin unrooted and three roots to federate.

❓ **Q5** - **How the identity substrate ships and who declares it at `infra`**: SPIRE, Istio, OpenBao, Pomerium and Dex are the only uncaged, unversioned population (H8-02). Versioned and platform-claimed is settled (principle 4, GAPS 3.14, ADR-0017); parent kind is closed (ticket 04); and under Q1 each adopter runs `spire-system` on its own cluster, where ticket 09 Q5 (a) says only a party with the `platform` role may declare a namespace at `infra`, but ADR-0018 makes the adopter's own Namespace manifest the declaration. Options: (a) the platform publishes the substrate as an `implementations` package with control claims (the COTS shim): the five HelmReleases plus the two ClusterSPIFFEIDs, self-versioned, gitsign-tagged; each org pins it, Renovate bumps it, Flux reconciles it; the `infra` declaration for `spire-system`, `istio-system`, `openbao` and `access` is rendered from the pinned platform package, so the platform's entitlement travels with the pin and the adopter's Namespace manifest for those namespaces is an output; (b) as (a) but the adopter declares those namespaces at `infra` on its own Namespace manifest, which ticket 09 Q5 (a) would render to `isolated`; (c) each org versions its own identity stack; the platform publishes only the SVID-path convention.

➡️ (a). It is what the code already calls itself (`identity/README.md:3-5`) and the class CONTEXT.md:224 already names; a version is the precondition for the cage because the claim the mutating policies key on is a version label. It turns a SPIRE chart bump into the reviewed PR that is the unit of adoption, moves the two ClusterSPIFFEIDs off kubectl (H8-14), and keeps ticket 04's kind closed. (b) collides with ticket 09 Q5 (a) and would isolate every adopter's SPIRE. (c) forks five charts three ways. Cross-reference ticket 09 Q5 for the ordering rule (the `infra` declaration lands and is asserted before the unlabelled default flips to `isolated`).
