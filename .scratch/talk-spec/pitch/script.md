### S1 — HOOK · "What does a breach cost?"
> What does a breach actually cost you? Not a red, amber, green dot on a dashboard. A number. In pounds. Because that number — not a compliance checkbox — is the only honest starting point for every security decision you will ever make.

### S2 — PROBLEM · governance is theatre
> Here's the uncomfortable truth. Most governance is theatre. Compliant, or not compliant. A green tick that means nothing moved, nobody priced the risk, and nobody can tell you what changed when the world did. It's a binary lie.

### S3 — THESIS · proportionate, informed, re-tuned
> So here's the idea I want to build. Governance is not a checkbox. It is a proportionate, informed, continuously re-tuned response to quantified risk. Version that whole chain, and proportionality stays honest as the world moves.

### S4 — REFRAME · policy is a versioned dependency
> Start with a reframe your engineers already trust. Policy is just a dependency. A lint pack. You pin it, you sign it, you adopt it by pull request. Governance becomes something a developer already knows how to consume.

### S5 — THE HOURGLASS
> The shape is an hourglass. Risk appetite, in pounds, sets the tone at the top. It funnels through principles, controls, and one enforcement decision. Then evidence flows back up into a single, defensible number. Versioned, end to end.

### S6 — MONEY-SHOT setup · two institutions
> Now the moment that makes the whole thesis land. Two institutions. A retailer, and a hospital. Same platform, same rule, same engine. One control: encrypt data at rest. Watch what proportionality actually does to it.

### S7 — MONEY-SHOT payoff · Audit vs Deny
> In the retailer, that control merely audits. In the hospital, the identical control blocks. Why? Because a health breach costs roughly eight times a retail one. Same rule, opposite verdict, because the pounds say so. Proportionality you can see.

### S8 — THE £ IS REAL · FAIR engine
> And the pounds are real. A lightweight actuarial engine takes calibrated estimates, runs a Monte Carlo, and returns expected loss and the tail — the bad year the board actually fears. It is the insurance industry's own maths.

### S9 — THE £ MOVES
> Best part: the number moves. Tighten a control, the pounds fall. Accept a risky condition, they rise. A new threat lands, they jump. Two runs, one input changed, and you watch the cost of a decision, live.

### S10 — CONDITIONAL POLICY · exemptions dissolved
> We kill the ugliest word in governance: exemption. No carve-outs, no favours. Instead: you may do this, if you meet these conditions. Uniform, versioned, priced. Every allowance carries its own honest line of accepted risk.

### S11 — SHIFT-LEFT
> Failures move left. The cluster advertises which policy versions it supports. Your pipeline runs the exact same check the cluster will, offline, before you merge. A deploy-time surprise becomes practically unheard of. The compliant path becomes the easy path.

### S12 — THE LIVING LOOP · war-gamer
> Then it comes alive. The estate war-games itself. Threat feeds, vulnerabilities, end-of-life dates, regulator penalties, market intelligence — all pour in. An agent stress-tests your controls and asks: is this still proportionate, or has the world moved?

### S13 — PROPOSE, NEVER DISPOSE
> When it drifts, the agent does one thing. It opens a pull request. It proposes. It never disposes. A human reviews, the gate checks, and only then does it ship. The scary capability is safe because it rides your existing rails.

### S14 — PROVENANCE · verify, don't trust
> And every actor, human or machine, signs their work. Cryptographically signed, publicly logged, verifiable end to end. You can prove which actor proposed what, when, and from what evidence. For an A.I.-enabled org, that is how you trust the machine.

### S15 — SIX ORGS, ALL LIVE
> None of this is slideware. Six real organisations. A shared platform, three live institutions, two regulators publishing real controls and real fines as code. A separate cluster each. Every claim in the talk is demonstrated live, not narrated.

### S16 — BALANCE SHEET close
> Which lets us close where no security talk closes: the balance sheet. Residual risk becomes economic capital. An underwriter would price the very same controls. Technological risk becomes a line your board can read, defend, and act on.
