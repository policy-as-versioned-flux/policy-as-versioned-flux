# L23 - ico (whole repository, 6,498 lines)

Verifier: read-only, 2026-11-14, against `fresh/ico` (main 9d584cf) and its consumers in `fresh/platform`, `fresh/driftwood`, `fresh/tuppence`, `fresh/ludlow`, `fresh/insurer`, `fresh/hub`. Arithmetic is mine, done in python3 on figures read from the files; it uses the closed-form PERT mean (lo + 4 x mode + hi) / 6, which is what the target loop uses.

Terms. *Converter*: `ico/schema/to_fair_scenario.py`, which turns one fine entry into a loss range. *Lm triple*: the (lowest, most likely, highest) loss per event. *Pin*: the tag and commit an adopter consumes, in `driftwood/gitops/flux-system/gotk-sync-ico.yaml`.

## Attempt 1: the loop runs without ico

What I could show.

- Nothing in the target's platform code would import ico. Today the coupling is a subprocess, not an import: `platform/compose/composition.py:3492-3504` runs ico's own `schema/to_fair_scenario.py build uk-gdpr lower-tier --turnover ...` from the pinned checkout (`_converter`, `:831-840`). The target replaces `composition.py` with a new `loop.py`, so the code path goes.
- The legacy fallback in `composition.py:776-779` (reads `schema/<version>/penalty-schema.json` "until ico v3 lands") is dead: v3 has an envelope at `ico/penalty-schema/v3/feed.json`, and the target drops `composition.py` anyway.
- ico's daily clock reacts to nothing. `ico/.github/workflows/fetch.yml:23-28` says "this repository ships no upstream fetcher yet"; the step at `:70-133` hashes ico's OWN published payload and appends a line to `observations`. 75 observation commits, latest 2026-11-14 (`git log origin/observations`). No regulator register is read anywhere in the repo.
- Every other consumer of ico is parked in the target: tuppence and ludlow (`tuppence/party.yaml:45`, `ludlow/party.yaml:46`), the insurer (`insurer/pricing/quote.py:497`), and nine hub verify scripts (`hub/verify/{pound-seam,priced-holes,feed-contract,e2e,...}`), which are the truth surface.

Where it fails. The loop's code would run, but its compare step would lose its meaning.

- Exposure in the target is, per row, mean events a year x (mean direct loss + penalty rate x mean penalty at the adopter's turnover). ico is the only source of the penalty term. No other feed carries a fine formula (F1 map table, lines 88-100). A case-insensitive grep for `penalty_rate` or `penalty-rate` across all nine `fresh/` clones finds no hit.
- For driftwood the penalty term is almost all of the figure. With the threat row (2, 4, 9) events and (1,000, 4,000, 9,000) direct loss, direct exposure is GBP 19,500. The penalty term at a penalty rate of 1 is GBP 3,706,001 on the v3 pin (verdict L11 reached GBP 3,725,501 including the direct term). Today's composition says the same: 98.9% of driftwood's priced subtotal is the ico line (`driftwood/composed/HEADER.yaml:1368`; A4 map row 3).
- What happens without ico depends on how it is removed. There are three cases, and none of them gives a working loop.
  - (a) ico is gone but the target still names it as a publisher. The feed cannot be read, so the target's NO MEASUREMENT rule applies: could-not-look, no verdict, no move, red after 10 minutes, every cycle. The loop never decides.
  - (b) ico is dropped from the design and the penalty term leaves the formula. driftwood's figure becomes GBP 19,500 against a tolerance of GBP 40,000, and the loop says a false `within`. It hides a loss that the estate's own figures put at about GBP 1.36 to 3.7 million a year.
  - (c) The penalty number is treated as a missing value on each row. The figure becomes a lower bound, and the verdict is `within-partial`: it never releases and never tightens.

Conclusion: attempt 1 fails. ico is not on the target's import chain, but it is on its data chain, and it supplies the term that the compare step turns on. I record `loop_runs_without_it: no`. Case (a) is the honest reading of the target as written, and case (b) is worse than not running.

## Attempt 2: the loop breaks or loses something without ico

This attempt succeeds. What lives only here.

1. **The only machine-readable fine, and the owner's own idea.** "the ico might be able to publish a machine readable financial penalty" (owner, 2026-07-23T16:43, A1 map line 55); "change the regulator financial fine ... and then make the controls ... proportionate" (2026-07-23T15:29); "GDPR style percentage of global revenue" (2026-08-19). ico is the one publisher with the owner's dated words behind it.

2. **The statutory cap rule.** `ico/schema/to_fair_scenario.py:234-248`: the ceiling is `max(cap, rate x turnover)`, the greater of the fixed sum and the percentage (UK GDPR Art 83(4)/(5), DPA 2018 s157). Without it, the ratio scaling at `:230-233` prices fines the statute does not allow for any firm with turnover above cap/rate. Introduced in a2fc5c7 (2026-09-09, ticket 79). It does not bite for driftwood: cap/rate is GBP 435,000,000 for lower tier and GBP 437,500,000 for higher tier, against driftwood's GBP 86,000,000. It did matter for ludlow: v3's high end would be GBP 3,079,766,559 against a statutory maximum of GBP 2,956,295,354. It costs about 4 lines.

3. **The finality rule: price the final collected figure, not the notice.** `ico/penalty-schema/rule.yaml:12-40` and `to_fair_scenario.py:79-175`. Lessons in the code and commits. A priced fine with no `source` is refused by name (937e0e8, review F1: an invented GBP 5,000,000 had moved the priced mode from GBP 92,000 to GBP 2,546,000 "and every check said PASS"). A half-graded set of examples is refused (`:134-139`). Doorstep Dispensaree's GBP 275,000 notice became the GBP 92,000 the tribunal set; Clearview AI's GBP 7,552,800 was never collected, so it does not price.

4. **No frequency without a basis.** `to_fair_scenario.py:178-203`: a frequency with no dated basis refuses (ADR-0020). This is the target's "one rule on numbers" applied at the publisher.

5. **The tags adopters pin.** v1.0.0, v3.0.0 (9d09222), v4.0.0 (9653fd9) and v4.0.1 (8e53a59). driftwood pins v3.0.0 by tag and commit (`driftwood/gitops/flux-system/gotk-sync-ico.yaml:33-35`). Its workflows check ico out at that pin (`driftwood/.github/workflows/propose-tier.yml:171-189`, `shift-left.yml:390-414`), and Renovate bumps the pair (`driftwood/renovate.json:72-80`).

6. **A trust anchor that names a file path.** driftwood accepts an ico tag only if it was signed by `policy-as-versioned-ico/ico/.github/workflows/cut-release.yml@refs/heads/(main|release/x.y.x)` (`gotk-sync-ico.yaml:26`; same regexp in `ico/.github/workflows/release.yml:57`). If a shrink renames or folds `cut-release.yml`, every new ico tag fails verification at the adopter. Lessons in the commits: 8902b66 (anchor the identity as a regexp so a backport branch's tag still verifies), 1abbc3d (an adopter's proposer identity must never verify ico's tag), and 816ef89 (a push by GITHUB_TOKEN does not trigger the release workflow, so `release.yml` needs a `workflow_dispatch` path).

### Tensions the target's authors should see

- **Tier choice.** driftwood prices `uk-gdpr/lower-tier` because `platform/compose/composition.py:3481-3482` hard-codes it, citing "spec.md's own acceptance wording", not a statute or a workload fact. The target says "an uncited fine tier is the higher tier". Applied as written, driftwood's penalty term rises from about GBP 1.36 million to about GBP 16.3 million at a penalty rate of 1. Either the tier gets a citation in `party.yaml`, or the higher tier applies.
- **Which frequency.** v4 publishes a per-regime frequency (0, 2, 4) with an editorial basis. The target takes events from the feeds threat row. Pick one. Applying both would double-count.
- **A correction that lowers the figure.** Moving from v3 to v4 cuts driftwood's penalty term by about 63% with no measured closure. The target's rule says only a measured closure may lower the figure. I read the rule as covering unsourced numbers, and the v4 figures are sourced corrections, so I think the move is allowed. But the target should say so in writing, or a strict reading blocks it.
- **penalty_rate does not exist.** No file in any of the nine clones carries it. "Counted with its basis or absent" means new work: a fetch that counts a regulator register (incidents fined out of incidents reported). That fetch does not exist. ico's clock hashes its own payload (`fetch.yml:23-28`, `:70-133`). Until someone builds the counter, the rate is absent and treated as 1, which is the right default.
- **ico inherits nist.** `ico/party.yaml:16-17` pins nist 1.1.0 because the v3/v4 `control_weights` key on nist control ids. nist is parked in the target. A v5 must drop `control_weights` or carry the few ids as plain strings. The release gate `verify-penalty-feed.sh:21-29` also reads `../platform/fair/fair.py` at a pinned platform v2.0.1 (`release.yml:55-56`), so it cannot be the release gate for a v5 judged by the new loop.

## Recommendation: shrink

**The 400-line budget.** The budget does not add up if ico keeps its converter. The converter core (about 220) + a uk-gdpr-only payload (about 103) + `cut-release.yml` (116) + `release.yml` (137) + `party.yaml` (29) comes to about 605 lines. That is before any payload schema, `rule.yaml` or fetch. The target's own text says `platform/loop.py` "prices each published loss row", and its ico entry names only "penalty feed v5 with a penalty-rate field; fetch and release". I read that as: pricing moves to platform, and ico becomes data plus release. On that reading the cap rule, the finality rule and the frequency-basis refusal are **must-absorb for platform's `loop.py`**, not keep-items for ico. The budget then closes at roughly 100-150 (payload) + 30-60 (schema) + about 40 (finality text in `rule.yaml`) + about 200 (two workflows, slimmed) + about 20 (`party.yaml`). The fetch that counts a register is extra and does not exist yet.
