## What the loop would lose if this area vanished

Today, nothing that moves a figure. No adopter amount, tolerance comparison or tier depends on any of these 77 files (paths A, C, E, F above).
What would genuinely be lost, all small:
- `calibration.md` is digest-pinned into the artefacts that sample a PERT triple (`pert.procedure`, `propagate.py:612`, `options.py:185`). Deleting it changes those artefact bytes and loses the one written discipline for authoring Q.
- `scoring.py` is the source copied into `nist/publisher-reliability/scoring.py`. The copy would keep working.
- `corroboration.py` would stop the hub `twin price` refusing mitigation credit for an unenacted control. That effect is on the hub twin, not on any adopter tier.
- `drift.py` and `verdict.py` are the instrument for the Flux thesis, which is a different question from the loop.
- The only live gap: nothing measures whether the quantified risk was right. Today no code here does that either, because there are no realised outcomes to score against (0 `outcomes/` directories; the incident-cost ledger in `platform/recovery-expense/` is prospective for 2027).

## The simplest thing that would do this area's loop job

The loop job of this area is: keep Q honest when the world changes, and widen or re-price when it does not hold. Concretely, about 300 lines, of which 236 exist.
1. Keep `twin/scoring.py` (167 lines) as the single proper-score function, and keep `twin/calibration.md` (69 lines) as the authoring discipline. Delete the nist copy and import the one.
2. Add one file per adopter, `twin/outcomes.jsonl`, one row per realised loss event: `{scenario_id, triple: [min, mode, max], window_end, realised_gbp, source}`. The source for real rows is the append-only ledger `platform/recovery-expense/collection.py` once it has entries.
3. Add one function, `interval_check(rows, n_min)` (about 40 lines in `scoring.py`): the fraction of rows whose realised value falls inside the stated 90% interval, with n reported, refusing below `n_min`. If coverage is below 0.9, emit a widening factor for the triple. This is the quantify step's feedback and uses the same "can only widen" rule `platform/compose/publisher_reliability.py` already applies.
4. The react step already exists: `tuppence/.github/workflows/propose-tier.yml` re-composes daily at today's date, and the compose step reads the widened triple. No forecasting, no derivation, no model judging is needed for the loop to run.
Everything else in the table (rows 2 to 10 and 12 to 25, about 27,200 lines) serves a question the loop does not ask.

## Surprises

1. 63% of the area (17,369 of 27,641 lines) landed in two days, 2026-10-30 and 2026-10-31, in PRs #287 to #303 on `calibration/*` and `source/*` branches (56 commits touching `twin/`). It produced an empty registry and, per its own contract text, "no implementation, acquisition, registration or permission". Of the 396 directories under `LOCAL/.scratch/calibration-programme/implementation-20261030` only 25 files exist, and `current-state`, `issues` and `review` are empty, so its working record is largely gone.
2. The word "calibration" carries three unrelated meanings here: PERT authoring discipline (`calibration.md`), forecast calibration (reliability diagram in `scoring.py`), and model qualification (the October programme). Only the first touches the money number.
3. `skill-scores.jsonl` is 20 rows: 14 heuristic keyword stand-ins scoring 1.0 against their own corpora, with fixed midnight timestamps on the first seven; the Laya rows (2026-09-21) are for a model whose probability head reads exactly 1.0 on all 279 calls (`model-head-readings.yaml:15-27`).
4. The derive-and-score machinery has a full pre-registration protocol (git first-parent committer dates, answer-key immutability, rename penalties) and zero data: no `*.forecast.yaml` anywhere, no outcome in any adopter, no tagged market or news feed (the feeds repo has 12 tags and none names either), and the market-moves feed says its prices are illustrative.
5. `twin/registration.py` (1,029 lines) is a ruling about what a `needs` edge may move and when a forecast question is registered; no forecast exists for it to govern.
6. The tuppence tier is the owner-adopted floor `isolated`, and the uncaged residual (1,289) is far under the 15,000 band (`HANDBOOK.md:84`). The loop's ratchet is never exercised in this record: the tier was set by a floor, not reached by tightening from a measured exposure above tolerance. This sits outside my area but bears on what "forecasting" could change.
7. The only path from a forecast score to a money amount is in `platform`/`nist`, was built by October work, and is dormant for want of both a declared policy and a population.
