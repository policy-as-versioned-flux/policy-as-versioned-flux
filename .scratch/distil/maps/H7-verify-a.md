## Summary

- Every line here is a verifier, not loop code. Nothing in this area quantifies, compares, ratchets or enacts: that code lives in platform (`composition.py`, `wargamer/tier_pr.py`, selection-policy), feeds and twin. Measured by role: nothing is `kernel`.
- The closest thing to the loop is `e2e` steps 2 to 4 (reprice, band crossing, Flux reconciles cage): about 1,100 lines, and its PASS lines say SYNTHETIC (steps 2 and 3 run on throwaway copies with a fabricated residual; step 4 on the runner is graded from a lane sample, not a cluster it looked at).
- About 13,200 of 19,120 lines (69%) grade documents, records, statuses, TRUTH-line citations, ADR banners, tickets, the map, handbooks, licences, an old org's archive, migrated demo apps, the local agent clock, or other checks. About 2,150 lines (branch-refs, forge-review, e2e step 6, feed-contract) grade supply-chain authenticity.
- About 3,800 lines (cve-inventory, engine-pairing, e2e steps 1 to 5, fold-agreement, cloud-plane) touch money arithmetic or a decision or cluster state the loop owns, and almost all of that grades planted or synthetic subjects.
- Header comments are heavy: 2,000+ `#` lines plus long Python docstrings explaining what each check refuses; the biggest single file is `cited-truth/cited_truth.py` (1,430 lines) which checks that tickets cite real TRUTH lines.
- The gate itself ran on 2026-11-12 (run 491): pass=117 fail=0 skip=26; every script here is PASS in `_grades.tsv` except three SKIPs (`deny-is-not-a-rung`, `e2e` step 5, `local-clock`).

## What the loop would lose if this area vanished

- Nothing the loop does. No code here quantifies, compares, ratchets or enacts; the loop's behaviour is in platform, feeds and twin.
- It would lose the only end-to-end demonstration that a re-pricing moves money, that a band crossing picks a tighter tier, and that a pod wears the tier (e2e steps 2 to 4, about 1,100 lines), plus the check that a CVE line equals the scanned intersection (108 lines) and that an unsupported engine is priced rather than refused (engine pairing). Those are demonstrations on synthetic or planted subjects, not observations of a real loop run.
- It would lose supply-chain authenticity checks (signed tags, anchored identities, reviewed-PR rulesets, Rekor): about 1,200 lines (branch-refs, forge-review, e2e step 6), plus feed-contract's 946. These make the evidence trustworthy; they do not run the loop.
- It would lose the honesty machinery that stops a green hiding a skip (lib-observation, every-green, e2e step 7, a-fall-blocks, can-record): about 2,000 lines that protect the record about the build, not the cage.

## The simplest thing that would do this area's loop job

One script, `verify/loop/verify-loop.sh` (about 150 to 250 lines, one Python helper reusing platform's composer and `tier_pr.py` as e2e steps 2 and 3 already do), run on the scheduled runner against the three adopters and asserting exactly the loop's steps, each with an exit code:

1. Q and X: take an adopter's committed tree, bump one pinned feed to its next version, compose, and require `prices[]` total to change (what `step2_reprice.py`, 205 lines, does).
2. T and C: read the adopter's signed `appetite.tolerance`; place a residual above and below it; require the selection to differ (the core of `step3_band.py`, about 150 of its 662 lines once the floor counterfactual, second implementation of the manifest finder and the 400-line selfcheck are dropped).
3. R and E: require the proposer to land a tighter tier only (never loosen) in a dry-run PR, then require on a cluster that the namespace declares the tier and a Running pod wears it (the seven facts of step 4, which can be four).
4. One line printed: `exposure=<GBP> tolerance=<GBP> tier=<name> within=<yes|no>`, appended to a log; that single line replaces the `TRUTH` line's role for the loop.

Everything else in this area (about 15,000 lines) is either a check on the checks, a check on documents about the build, or a check on housekeeping for an old org and demo apps.

## Surprises

- The area has no kernel. "verify" is 54 entries in the hub (half of them here) yet the compare/ratchet code is imported from platform; the hub only grades it.
- `e2e` steps 2 and 3 say SYNTHETIC in their own PASS lines: step 2 composes a throwaway copy with a made-up pin bump, step 3 uses a residual "not driftwood's real priced position". The last capture also records that the one real merged Renovate bump (driftwood #20, threat-register v1 to v2) "moved the recorded pin and no money". The adopter's own declared tier (`isolated`) is already tighter than what the priced line selects (`restricted`), so on the real estate the proposer HOLDS; the ratchet's tighten branch is only exercised on a copy the check rewrites to `baseline`.
- `engine-pairing` prices an unsupported engine at the sum of claimed controls' hole prices, but on the estate's real claims that amount is `null` (only 2 controls are claimed and neither is weighted by the regulator); only a planted weighted claim moves a number (engine-pairing/README.md:38).
- `local-clock` (1,479 lines): the owner asked for the clock scripts themselves (Q10, 2026-09-02, quote in the table), not for this check of them. No `.local-clock/last-run.json` exists at LOCAL/.local-clock and its gate row is SKIP on the runner; it is exercised only against stub `claude` and `gh`. What it mostly polices is the model's behaviour (signing, tags, hooks, remotes), i.e. a cage on the twin agent, not on the organisation's risk.
- Five components are about the record of the build rather than the cage: `cited-truth` (1,569 lines, the largest), `derived-status`, `map-surface`, `adr-supersession`, `a-fall-blocks`; `can-record` and `every-green` guard the record's honesty. Together about 4,300 lines (5,700 with `can-record` and `every-green`) grade tickets, ADRs, the map and the TRUTH log.
- `lifted-apps` (1,878 lines) and `handbook` (1,037) are migration and documentation bookkeeping for apps and a page the owner's loop never reads; `incumbent-org` (378) governs archiving an old GitHub org.
- Five components (cve-inventory, cloud-plane, engine-pairing, enact-record, incumbent-org) were born in the single 2026-10-03 commit 6ed89616 ("Resume Claude ecosystem handoff with measured implementation and delivery proofs", 611 files, 366,822 insertions). Their dense semicolon style differs from the verbose September checks, which suggests a different builder, but I found no recorded agent: a grep for "codex" over the commit messages of `verify/` returns 0, all 251 commits in `verify/` carry the owner's git name (236) or the bot (15), and the owner's local `resume-2026-10-17/` directory holds working material for tickets 151, 142 and others without naming an agent. Their ASK is recorded as delegated (ticket 153 quotes the owner's 2026-10-03 delegation; ADR-0025 delegation otherwise starts 2026-09-02 per ticket 75 Q11); builder identity is could-not-determine. enact-record's shape was a HITL ticket (97), so its origin is `unknown`.
- Many selfchecks plant violations to prove the guard bites; the header comment density (about 160 of 403 lines in a-fall-blocks, 326 of 1,722 in e2e) means the explanation of each check is often longer than its logic.
- Tests for most of these also live outside the area (`tests/test_can_record.py`, `test_cited_truth.py`, `test_deny_register.py`, `test_local_clock.py`, `test_lifted_apps.py`, and others), so the true size of the verification layer is larger than the 19,120 lines counted here.
