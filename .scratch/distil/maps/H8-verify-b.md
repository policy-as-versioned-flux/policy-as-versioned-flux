## Summary

- 85 files, 20,677 lines (wc -l, summed per directory), all of it CHECKERS. There is no engine in this area: the money maths (fair.py, enforce.py, cage.py select_tier) and the cage bodies live in platform; these directories grade them or grade documents about them. No component here is kernel: the loop runs if every directory below is deleted (verified by reading: the proportionality README says it "reuses platform/graded/cage.py ... no risk engine ... is reimplemented here", hub/verify/proportionality/README.md:66-69).
- Closest to the loop: pound-seam (arithmetic of the quantified exposure, 2,275 lines), tier-binding (enacted tier is at least the priced tier, 480), priced-holes (holes are priced, 1,048), proportionality (band alone moves the rung, a talk demo, 498), twin-cage (cages the twin agent's write credential, not a workload, 1,211). All five are support at best; proportionality is a presentation beat.
- Recorded observations (hub/talk/captures/_grades.tsv, TRUTH run 491, 2026-11-12) show the loop's ratchet is exhausted: tier-binding reads all three adopters declaring `isolated` (the bottom rung) with every priced line also `isolated`; pound-seam reads tuppence serving a 102,869,480.25 GBP total against a 15,000 GBP tolerance, labelled "an ordinal, auditable comparison ... not an expected annual loss" (owner "yes", ticket 75 Q4). So Q is disclaimed as money, C is a comparison of ordinals, and R has nowhere left to go.
- About a quarter of the lines are ceremony, off-mission or presentation: record, truth-line, unreviewed-major, model-permission, misuse, sensor-admission, twin-evals, twin-per-adopter, proportionality (4,795 lines), plus the 480-line provenance narrative (5,275, 25.5 percent). A further large share is planted-defect selfcheck fixtures inside the checkers themselves (pound_seam.py 561 of 2,219 lines are `_good`/selfcheck, hub/verify/pound-seam/pound_seam.py:1659-2219; schedules.py 556 of 1,980, hub/verify/schedules/schedules.py:1410-1966).
- Evidence of running is good: all 26 directories have a PASS or declared-SKIP line in the 2026-11-12 grade table. Evidence of the loop acting on a cluster is thin: only served-workloads/oscal-lane (62 lines of wrapper over adopter-held instruments) reads recorded cluster samples; the proportionality live tails are `never` (no kind clusters on the runner).

## The five checks closest to the loop, in plain words

1. pound-seam (hub/verify/pound-seam/pound_seam.py). Reads each adopter's composed/evidence.json and asks: is every price one currency and one perspective; do the parts add up to the total; is the appetite the adopter's own signed number; do two separate copies of the selection logic (platform's cage.py and the adopter's package) pick the same rung at every band boundary; does a served total say it is an ordinal comparison. It never asks whether the exposure is TRUE as a financial figure, and the owner said it need not be (ticket 75 Q4 "yes" to the ordinal reading). Class: arithmetic of the loop, over committed artefacts, not a cluster. The 10 SKIPs name prices with no amount (418 scanned CVEs in no pinned KEV feed; switching costs with "supplies no lef").
2. proportionality (hub/verify/proportionality/verify-proportionality.sh). Asks: with one fixed scenario, does a 40,000 GBP band choose baseline and a 5,000 GBP band choose quarantine, and does the shared cage-tier policy then dress two pods differently. It is a compare-plus-ratchet demonstration on a hand-typed triple (the "uncaged residual" £21,360 is arithmetic on `lef [1,2,5]` and `lm [3000,8000,20000]` in the scenario file). The real adopters do not behave this way: tier-binding shows all three at `isolated`. Class: arithmetic plus a CLI stand-in; the cluster legs are `never` on the runner.
3. tier-binding (hub/verify/tier-binding/tier_binding_estate.py). Asks: is the tier written on each governed Namespace manifest at least as tight as the strictest tier its own evidence prices. It passes today because declared = priced = `isolated` for every adopter (a one-rung ladder end), so it cannot currently be seen to bite except through its selfcheck plants. Class: arithmetic over committed manifests; not what is running on a cluster.
4. priced-holes (hub/verify/priced-holes/priced_holes.py). Asks: when composition meets a new hole, does it print a priced delta instead of refusing, and does the ungoverned-namespace price recompute from manifests, tags and the EOL ramp. 284 holes per adopter, 4 priced; the other 280 are `amount: null` with a named limit. Class: arithmetic.
5. twin-cage (hub/verify/twin-cage/twin_cage.py). Asks: does the twin's scheduled workflow give its writer job only the permissions its priced rung permits, with the read-only twin job unable to write. Class: workflow YAML plus one dated receipt; it protects the build's own AI agent, which is process around the loop, not the cage on a workload.

## What the loop would lose if this area vanished

- Nothing it needs to run. The quantify (fair.py), compare and ratchet (cage.py select_tier, wargamer.select_party_tier), enact (tier_pr.py, cage-tier policy) and react (Renovate, scheduled clocks) are all outside this area, in platform, the adopters and .github. I grepped the hub for references to pound_seam and tier-binding outside their own directories and found none; the dependency arrows run from these scripts into platform, not back (other directories' inbound references were not searched).
- What it would lose is detection of silent drift in three places: a composed price that mixes currencies or whose parts do not sum (pound-seam, priced-holes, supersede); an adopter that declares a tier looser than it priced (tier-binding); an adopter gate that stops verifying a real signature or whose Sigstore root goes stale (real-signature, trust-root). Those are the "can this be trusted" checks the loop needs once it is wired to real money; today the quantities they protect are labelled ordinal.
- It would also lose the only recorded cluster-facing observation in this area (served-workloads and oscal-lane, 62 lines of wrapper over adopter-held instruments) and the one recorded proof that the react step fired once (renovate, 61 lines).
- Everything else (record, truth-line, misuse, model-permission, unreviewed-major, sensor-admission, twin-evals, twin-per-adopter, proportionality, the provenance narrative, 5.3k lines) is process about the build or a talk beat. The loop loses nothing.

## The simplest thing that would do this area's loop job

One script, `verify/loop_check.py`, about 150 to 200 lines, plus the two cluster wrappers kept as they are (62 lines):

1. For each adopter, load `party.yaml` for `appetite.tolerance` and currency, `composed/evidence.json` for `prices[]`, and the declared tier from `gitops/apps/namespace.yaml`.
2. Assert the minimum arithmetic: one currency; `sum(lines) == total`; no mixed-currency sum (the three load-bearing assertions of pound-seam checks 1, 4, 5, about 40 lines).
3. Call `cage.select_tier(residuals, tolerance, floor)` once and assert `declared_tier >= selected_tier` (the whole of tier-binding, about 30 lines, because the two-implementation comparison disappears if there is one implementation).
4. Print one line: `exposure / tolerance / tier / headroom`, and exit non-zero when exposure > tolerance and tier < isolated (a ratchet is available but not taken). This line is the C step the area never states plainly today.
5. Read the last TRUTH line and fail if older than the clock period (the useful 20 lines of schedules.py, without the 556-line selfcheck).
6. Keep `served-workloads` and `renovate` unchanged.

That replaces roughly 13,600 lines of loop-adjacent checkers (pound-seam, tier-binding, priced-holes, supersede, portability, schedules, refusal-by-another-name, publisher-reliability, twin-cage, party) with about 200, at the price of losing selfcheck-plants and signature/trust-root authenticity checks, which belong in platform's own repo gate (they already exist there, hub/talk/verify-manifest.txt:134,139,144).
