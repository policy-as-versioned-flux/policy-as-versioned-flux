# The proof on a cluster that stays up, 15 November 2026

This is the record of the first proof of the loop on a cluster that outlives a cycle. It ran on 2026-11-15, from an agent session, on a kind cluster on the owner's own machine. Its eight lines are in `lines.jsonl`, next to this file, one JSON object per line, in the shape of driftwood's ledger. Every value in the table of eight lines below is taken from that file. What the lines do not carry (pod limits, start times, restart counts, the refused step) was seen in the session and is marked so. All times are UTC on 2026-11-15.

It is recorded as K19 in `CONTRACT.md`, delegated, applying the owner's answer 3: the proof on a cluster that stays up runs from an agent session on the owner's machine, with the pinned harness and loop unchanged; its lines say `cluster: standing` and go to no ledger; Flux stays out.

## Words

`CONTRACT.md` and `CONTEXT.md` define these. In brief:

- **Rung.** A named cage setting. There are three, loosest first: `baseline` closes nothing; `quarantine` closes `egress` (the probe pod cannot reach out); `isolated` also closes `inbound` (nothing reaches the pods, so the shop does not serve). The cage is two Kyverno policies. One stamps each pod with its rung and sets its limits; the other writes a network rule, `cage-reach-<rung>`, for `quarantine` and `isolated`. The first reads the rung from the Namespace's label. The second acts on the label the first has put on the pod, and each rule it writes selects pods by that label. Both act when a pod is created or updated.
- **Exposure** is the expected loss in pounds a year, summed over the rows that are open. A row is closed only when a capability it needs is measured closed and the rung declares it closed. **Cost** is the current rung's running cost a year. **Figure** is exposure plus cost. **Tolerance** is the loss a year the adopter says it will bear.
- **Verdicts** seen here: `within` (figure at or below tolerance, hold); `tighten` (a tighter rung is the goal, move one notch); `saturated` (figure above tolerance and no move helps, red); `cage-ineffective` (a capability the rung declares closed is measured open, red, every row priced open).
- **Release-ready**: some looser rung would still be within the tolerance less the margin of 20%. A release needs release-ready ledger lines for the 7-day dwell.

## What decision 9 asked for, and why

Decision 9 of `DISTILLATION.md` section 7 asked where the cage runs. Its reason, in its own words: "only a cluster that outlives a cycle shows a process that was already running being re-caged". The daily loop builds a new kind cluster for every cycle and throws it away, so every pod it sees was created at the rung in git. It never sees a running pod moved from one rung to another. The options were (a) a cluster that stays up, with a runner beside it, and (b) the daily throwaway cluster only. The recommendation was (b) now, and (a) for the proof and the break test.

The owner answered "Ok" on 2026-11-14 (`DECISIONS.md`, row 9). On 2026-11-15 the owner chose where it runs: a kind cluster on the owner's own machine, left running (`DECISIONS.md`, "15 November: the four answers", answer 3). The option said, before the owner chose it, that GitHub's runners cannot reach that machine, so the proof and the break test would run from a session and be recorded as such. There is no runner beside the cluster.

## The step with no line: a refused tightening

At about 10:38, between `c3` and `d2`, the pods were replaced at `baseline`: deleted and created again while the Namespace said `baseline`. So they were created with `baseline`'s limits, 500m CPU and 256Mi memory, and with no capability dropped. The Namespace label was then set to `isolated` and each pod was annotated, as in the runs above.

The API server refused every update to those pods. The cage's mutation at `isolated` lowers the limits and drops capabilities, and a running pod's spec may not change in that way. The refusal, in the API server's own words, in part: "pod updates may not change fields other than `spec.containers[*].image`…" and "must be less than or equal to cpu limit of 100m". The second refers to the CPU limit of 100m that `isolated` sets.

The pods stayed labelled `baseline`. No cage network rule selected them. No cycle was run on this state, so it has no line. The next run, `d2`, replaced the pods with the Namespace at `isolated`: they came up with limits of 100m and capabilities dropped (seen in the session), and the line reads both closed, serving 0 of 3, `saturated`.

## What it shows

1. **The break test, for real, on a cluster that was already running.** Deleting the network rule was seen by the next probe. The figure went from 1,643,518.63 to 17,924,390.06 and the verdict to `cage-ineffective`, while git and the labels still said `isolated`. The money follows the measurement, not the label.
2. **A process that was already running is re-caged without a restart**, from `isolated` to `quarantine` (`c1`) and back to `isolated` by way of `baseline` (`c3`), and the network rule is written again on any pod update. The cage acts on a pod update, so something must touch the pod. Here it was an annotation.
3. **On the new pins the loop is green on this cluster too.** `standing-e1-new-pins` reads `within` at GBP 17,290.68 against GBP 430,000, release-ready, as ledger line 11 does.

## Findings

These two are not fixed.

1. **A climb from `baseline` cannot be made in place.** A pod created at `baseline` cannot be tightened while it runs: the update is refused, and until the pod is replaced it has `baseline`'s reach whatever the Namespace says. Worse, every update to such a pod is refused while the Namespace sits at a tighter rung. On a cluster that stays up, a climb from `baseline` therefore needs the pods replaced. (`c3` went from `baseline` back to `isolated` in place, but its pods had been created at `isolated`. The reading here, not checked in the session, is that by finding 2 they still had `isolated`'s limits and dropped capabilities, so the move asked for no change the API server refuses.)
2. **Loosening to `baseline` in place is partial.** The label and the network rule follow. The limits and the dropped capabilities stay as the pod was created. (`c2` read both capabilities open and serving 3 of 3, which is what the loop measures. It does not measure limits.)

The daily loop is not affected by either. It builds a new cluster every cycle, so every pod is created at the rung in git.

Closing them needs a change to the cage (a new cage version, a tag and a pin change), or Deployments with a rollout on each rung change. `CONTRACT.md` section 10 already names Deployments and a rollout stamp as the work of the cluster that stays up. That work was not in the scope of the owner's answer, and was not done.

*Added later on 2026-11-15.* Finding 1 was fixed the same day by cage 8.0.2 (platform `policy/v8.0.2`), which sets only the labels on a pod update; see `2026-11-15-b.md`. Finding 2 stands, by design: a pod moved in place keeps its created limits and capabilities until it is replaced.

## Limits

- It ran from an agent session, by hand, not from `loop.yml`. GitHub's runners cannot reach the owner's machine. The owner was told this before choosing it.
- Flux is not installed (`CONTRACT.md` K5 stands). The cluster does not follow git. After the release expected on 2026-11-22 it will still be at `isolated` until someone syncs it from a session.
- It stops when the machine or Docker stops.
- Lines `c1` and `c2` were run with a copy of the namespace file at the rung under test, because git says `isolated`.
- The seven lines before `e1` compare against the fixture tolerance of GBP 40,000, because they ran before the owner's tolerance landed.
- The node is arm64, and two of the three app images ran under emulation.
- The lines go to no ledger. They are evidence of what the cage does to running pods, not part of the loop's record.

## How to look at the cluster, and how to remove it

From the top of the hub's working copy on the owner's machine, with Docker running:

```sh
export KUBECONFIG="$PWD/.standing/kubeconfig"
kubectl --context kind-loop -n driftwood get pods -L posture.acme.io/tier
kubectl --context kind-loop -n driftwood get networkpolicy
```

To remove it, with the same `KUBECONFIG`:

```sh
kind delete cluster --name loop
```
