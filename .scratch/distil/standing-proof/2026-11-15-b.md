This is the record of the second proof of the loop on a cluster that outlives a cycle. Its three lines are in `lines-b.jsonl`, next to this file, one JSON object per line, in the shape of driftwood's ledger. Every value in the table of three lines below is taken from that file. What the lines do not carry (updates admitted, pod identities, start times, restart counts, limits and capabilities) was seen in the session and is marked so. All times are UTC on 2026-11-15.

The first proof, earlier the same day, is `2026-11-15.md`. Its rules still hold: the proof runs from an agent session with the pinned harness and loop unchanged, its lines say `cluster: standing`, and they go to no ledger (K19 in `CONTRACT.md`).

## Words

`CONTRACT.md` and `CONTEXT.md` define these. In brief:

- **Rung.** A named cage setting, loosest first: `baseline` closes nothing; `quarantine` closes `egress` (the probe pod cannot reach out); `isolated` also closes `inbound` (nothing reaches the pods, so the shop does not serve). The cage is two Kyverno policies. One reads the rung from the Namespace's label, stamps each pod with it and, when the pod is created, sets its limits and, at `quarantine` and `isolated`, drops its capabilities. The other writes a network rule, `cage-reach-<rung>`, for `quarantine` and `isolated`, which selects pods by the rung on their stamp.
- **Exposure** is the expected loss in pounds a year over the rows that are open. **Cost** is the current rung's running cost a year. **Figure** is exposure plus cost. **Tolerance** is the loss a year the adopter says it will bear. `within` means the figure is at or below the tolerance: hold.
- **Release-ready**: some looser rung would still be within the tolerance less the margin. A release needs release-ready ledger lines for the 7-day dwell.

## Why it was run

The first proof found two things.

1. **A climb from `baseline` could not be made in place.** The first proof tried to move pods created at `baseline` to `isolated`, by setting the Namespace and annotating each pod. The API server refused every update: the cage tried to lower a running pod's limits and drop its capabilities, and Kubernetes will not change those on a running pod. The pods kept `baseline`'s label and no cage network rule selected them.
2. **A pod moved in place keeps the limits and capabilities it was created with.** The label and the network rule follow the rung; the rest does not.

Cage 8.0.2, under platform tag `policy/v8.0.2`, answers the first. When a pod is created it does what cage 8.0.0 did. On an update it sets the two stamp labels only (`posture.acme.io/caged`, `posture.acme.io/tier`) and does not touch the pod's spec. So the update is admitted, the label follows, and the network rule follows the label. The second stands, by design (below).

This proof repeats the refused step on the cluster that stays up, with the new cage.

## What it shows

1. **Finding 1 is fixed by cage 8.0.2, on the cluster where it was found.** The climb from `baseline` to `isolated` that the API server refused under cage 8.0.0 was admitted. The running pods took the new label without a restart, and the probe read both capabilities closed and serving 0 of 3.
2. **The figure follows what the probe measures, not the limits.** On `f2` the pods still had `baseline`'s limits and capabilities, and the line reads the same as `f1` and `f3`. The loop measures reach and serving, not limits.

Platform's `harness/proof.sh` now proves the fixed step on every platform pull request and every merge to `main`. Its run 4, "a climb in place", creates the fixture's one pod at `baseline`, moves its Namespace to `quarantine` and makes one update to the running pod. The update must be admitted, the label must follow, the network rule must be written again, and the pod must not be replaced or restarted. Its CPU limit must still be 500m. The probe and the cycle must then read `egress` closed, `inbound` open, serving 1 of 1, exposure 1,000.00, figure 7,000.00, `within`. Cage 8.0.0 fails that run.

## What still stands

Finding 2, by design. After `f2` the pods were caged at `isolated` by the network rule, with `baseline`'s limits and capabilities, until `f3` replaced them. `quarantine` and `isolated` set the same limits and drop the same capabilities; `baseline` sets looser limits and drops none. So on a cluster that stays up, a rung change to or from `baseline` needs the pods replaced, so that they also take the new rung's limits and capabilities. This is a procedure, not code.

The daily loop is not affected by either finding: it builds a new cluster every cycle, so every pod is created at the rung in git.

## Limits

- It ran from an agent session, by hand, not from `loop.yml`. GitHub's runners cannot reach the owner's machine.
- Flux is not installed (`CONTRACT.md` K5 stands). The cluster does not follow git. After the release expected on 2026-11-22 it will still be at `isolated` until someone syncs it from a session, and a sync to `baseline` needs the pods replaced (finding 2).
- It stops when the machine or Docker stops.
- The lines go to no ledger. They are evidence of what the cage does to running pods, not part of the loop's record.
- What is marked as seen in the session is not in the lines and cannot be checked from them.
